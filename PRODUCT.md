# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Potential clients and employers evaluating Jamie's work. They arrive with a hiring or collaboration lens: scanning for craft, personality, and proof of range (web, AI, creative development). They are often time-poor, browsing on desktop or mobile between meetings, and deciding whether this person feels distinct enough to remember and reach out to.

## Product Purpose

jamjam.dev is Jamie's personal portfolio and blog. It showcases projects, writing, and Lab experiments while communicating who Jamie is as a creative developer, not just what he has shipped. Success looks like a visitor leaving with a clear sense of personality and technical confidence, enough to start a conversation about work. Secondary goals: surface blog posts and Lab tools that demonstrate ongoing curiosity and capability.

## Brand Personality

Technical, playful, confident. The terminal prompt motif (`jamjam:~$`) is voice, not costume: mono labels, status lines, and shell-style section headers should feel native to the site. Dark-first surfaces with green accent carry a dev-studio atmosphere without sliding into generic "dark mode SaaS." Humor and craft sit together; the site should feel like someone who builds things and enjoys the process.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS landing pages: cream backgrounds, hero metrics, identical feature card grids, buzzword copy
- Template portfolios: Inter or default sans stacks, purple gradients, same-sized project cards with icon + title + blurb repeated endlessly
- Corporate or agency brochures: stock photography, "we leverage synergies" language, personality replaced by credentials

## Design Principles

1. **Personality is the product.** Hiring visitors should leave knowing who Jamie is, not just what projects exist. Copy, motion, and layout serve character before conversion tricks.
2. **Refine, don't reinvent.** The dark terminal aesthetic is the committed identity. Improve hierarchy, contrast, spacing, and motion within that lane rather than chasing trend pivots.
3. **Terminal voice must be earned.** Mono prompts and shell metaphors belong where they add delight or wayfinding; avoid sprinkling `jamjam:~$` on every surface until it becomes noise.
4. **Show craft through work.** Projects, posts, and Lab experiments are the proof. Design frames the work; it does not replace it.
5. **Reject category defaults.** If a section could belong to any developer portfolio template, rewrite it until it could only belong here.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Sensible defaults: sufficient text contrast on dark surfaces, keyboard-navigable UI, semantic HTML. GSAP and motion are part of the brand; respect `prefers-reduced-motion` on animated heroes and transitions. No formal WCAG certification target, but avoid known failures (muted gray body text on near-black, motion-only information, inaccessible form controls).
