# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

People who hire or collaborate, and people who came for a tech post. Both should meet the same person: creative, a bit of a nerd, with proof of range (web, writing, pictures, film, graphics, music, AI, Lab tools). Time-poor, desktop or mobile, deciding whether this person is distinct enough to remember. A Next.js reader who also finds photography is not a wrong turn; a photographer who finds a custom marketing site should be able to feel the same.

## Product Purpose

jamjam.dev is Jamie's personal portfolio and blog. It showcases Projects, Posts, the Board, and Lab experiments, and communicates who Jamie is — not only what has shipped. The hero orients the mix once, in a human sentence, not a job title; then the work sits without apology. Success looks like a visitor leaving with a clear sense of personality and craft, enough to start a conversation. Posts stay writing about tech. The Board accompanies that; it does not replace it or take over the blog.

## Brand Personality

Technical, playful, confident — creative and a bit of a nerd. The terminal prompt motif (`jamjam:~$`) is voice, not costume: mono labels, status lines, and shell-style section headers should feel native to the site. Dark-first surfaces with green accent carry a dev-studio atmosphere without sliding into generic "dark mode SaaS." Humor and craft sit together; the site should feel like someone who builds things and enjoys the process.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS landing pages: cream backgrounds, hero metrics, identical feature card grids, buzzword copy
- Template portfolios: Inter or default sans stacks, purple gradients, same-sized project cards with icon + title + blurb repeated endlessly
- Corporate or agency brochures: stock photography, "we leverage synergies" language, personality replaced by credentials
- Two-shop portfolios: web over here, photography over there, or a fenced "also creative" aside that treats one body of work as a footnote

## Design Principles

1. **Personality is the product.** Visitors should leave knowing who Jamie is, not just what Projects exist. Copy, motion, and layout serve character before conversion tricks. Orient the mix once; do not split the site by audience.
2. **Refine, don't reinvent.** The dark terminal aesthetic is the committed identity. Improve hierarchy, contrast, spacing, and motion within that lane rather than chasing trend pivots.
3. **Terminal voice must be earned.** Mono prompts and shell metaphors belong where they add delight or wayfinding; avoid sprinkling `jamjam:~$` on every surface until it becomes noise.
4. **Show craft through work.** Projects, Posts, Board items, and Lab experiments are the proof. Design frames the work; it does not replace it. Projects are the main feature; the Board is a scrapbook, not a second case-study track.
5. **Reject category defaults.** If a section could belong to any developer portfolio template, rewrite it until it could only belong here.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Sensible defaults: sufficient text contrast on dark surfaces, keyboard-navigable UI, semantic HTML. GSAP and motion are part of the brand; respect `prefers-reduced-motion` on animated heroes and transitions. No formal WCAG certification target, but avoid known failures (muted gray body text on near-black, motion-only information, inaccessible form controls).
